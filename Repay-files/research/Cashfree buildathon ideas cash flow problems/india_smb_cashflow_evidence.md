# India SMB and Enterprise Finance / Cash-Flow Problems: Evidence Test of Reddit-Sourced Claims (state as of 27 Oct 2026)

Method note: each number is dated. Anything older than 2023 is flagged [PRE-2023]. Vendor and lender blogs are flagged [VENDOR]. Several pages were only readable as excerpts or via an extraction layer; where a figure's date is unclear, that is stated. The live MSME Samadhaan pending-amount page (samadhaan.msme.gov.in) failed to load, so Samadhaan figures come from CRISIL (data cut-off 14 Aug 2026) and a Businessworld article (undated).

## 1. Delayed payments to MSMEs in India: amounts, days, MSME Samadhaan, Section 43B(h), facilitation councils

### Takeaway
Delayed B2B payment is confirmed as a large, structural India problem, but the headline "56 days vs 27 days of cash" is a US figure and has no India equivalent. India evidence: formal Samadhaan claims of Rs 55,244 crore (Aug 2026, only the reported tip), Atradius says 63% of credit-based B2B sales are overdue and 7% become bad debt, and Parliament has just moved a 2026 amendment to the MSMED Act (passed the Rajya Sabha around 3 Aug 2026) because dispute resolution is slow. Section 43B(h) has been in force since FY2024-25, but I found no quantified evidence of its effect on actual payment days.

### Cited Findings
- MSME Samadhaan, data as of 14 Aug 2026: 2,56,892 applications on delayed payments, worth Rs 55,244 crore; Rs 20,979 crore still pending; about 40,580 applications (16%) unresolved for more than a year. CRISIL says many MSMEs do not formally report delays, so true delays may be "significantly higher" — [CRISIL Intelligence, 17 Aug 2026](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/newsroom/press-releases/2026/08/executed-well-msme-bill-can-be-an-ibc-moment-for-delayed-payments.html)
- Facilitation council load per council: Karnataka 397 applications on average (35 councils), Uttar Pradesh 1,095 (19 councils), Rajasthan 1,767 (9 councils) — [CRISIL, Aug 2026](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/newsroom/press-releases/2026/08/executed-well-msme-bill-can-be-an-ibc-moment-for-delayed-payments.html)
- Earlier Samadhaan snapshot (date not stated in the extract): Rs 21,108 crore pending, over 90,000 applications filed; 40,000 formalised as MSEFC cases worth Rs 13,199 crore; largest defaulters were state governments (Rs 3,170 crore) and CPSEs (Rs 2,191 crore) — [Businessworld](https://businessworld.in/article/pending-msme-payment-reaches-rs-21108-cr-data-537926). Date unknown, so treat as an older snapshot. It does show that public-sector buyers are a large part of the problem.
- MSMED (Amendment) Bill, 2026 provisions: mediation within 90 days; arbitration within 30 days of failed mediation; award within 90 days of pleadings; buyer must deposit 75% of an award before challenging it; at least 50% of the deposit released to the MSME if proceedings exceed six months; awards recoverable as arrears of land revenue and recognised as enforceable debt under insolvency law — [CRISIL, Aug 2026](https://intelligence.crisil.com/en/homepage/newsroom/press-releases/2026/08/executed-well-msme-bill-can-be-an-ibc-moment-for-delayed-payments.html)
- Bill status: reported passed by the Rajya Sabha, article dated 3 Aug 2026 (full text paywalled; Lok Sabha status and Presidential assent not confirmed by me) — [BCR Pub](https://bcrpub.com/news/indias-upper-house-clears-delayed-payment-bill-to-unlock-msme-cash/)
- Atradius India payment practices (page dated 16 Oct 2024, labelled "2025"; fieldwork dates not given): on average 63% of credit-based B2B sales are overdue; bad debt is 7% of B2B invoices; main causes are customer liquidity constraints and supply chain disruption; 72% of firms expect customer insolvencies to rise. By sector: agri-food 44% of sales on credit, more than 60% of invoices overdue, terms over 50 days; chemicals 52% on credit, more than 70% overdue, terms near 60 days; textiles 53% on credit, 56% overdue, terms 47 days, write-offs up to 10% — [Atradius Collections](https://atradiuscollections.com/hk/出版物/報告/b2b-payment-practices-trends-india-2025). The full PDF with DSO chart data could not be accessed.
- Legal rule: MSMED Act section 15 caps agreed credit at 45 days from acceptance (15 days if no written agreement); Section 43B(h) of the Income Tax Act, introduced by Finance Act 2023, denies a tax deduction for a delayed payment to a registered micro or small supplier until it is actually paid. Applicable from FY2023-24 (AY2024-25) per most explainers. One explainer says "April 1, 2024" for applicability, so the start year is ambiguous in secondary sources — [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/section-43bh-new-msme-45-days-payment-rule), [TwoTax, 7 Mar 2024](https://www.twotax.in/blog-details/new-msme-45-days-payment-rule-section-43b-h-). Interest under the MSMED Act is three times the RBI bank rate [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/msme-samadhaan-delayed-payment-recovery/).
- Reaction to 43B(h): industry body asked the finance minister to continue the rule (BFSI ET headline only, excerpt) [ET BFSI](https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/continue-45-day-msme-payment-rule-industry-body-to-fm/111741228); traders/MSME bodies reportedly appealed in the Supreme Court in May 2024 (headline-level only) [Business Standard, May 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/msmes-protest-45-day-payment-rule-file-appeal-in-apex-court-report-124050300544_1.html). I did not read the full articles.
- Older aggregate [PRE-2023]: delayed MSME payments of about Rs 10.7 lakh crore, with micro and small bearing about 80% (GAME and Dun & Bradstreet, 2022), as cited in the RXIL impact report — [RXIL Impact Assessment, May 2025](https://www.rxil.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/trade-receivables-impact-assessment-report-2025.pdf). Secondary citation; I did not find the original.

### Inferences
- Samadhaan's Rs 55,244 crore is a floor on reported disputes, not the stock of overdue invoices. The Rs 10.7 lakh crore (2022) and the Atradius 63% overdue rate point to a far larger stock, but the two are not comparable and I found no recent official total of overdue MSME receivables.
- The "56 days to get paid" claim is untested for India. Atradius gives sector terms of 47 to 60 days plus a month or more past due for some sectors, which suggests effective collection of well over 60 days in agri-food and chemicals, but this is my inference from partial data, not a measured DSO.
- The 2026 Bill focuses on enforcement against buyers who dispute, which confirms that delay is a legal-enforcement problem and not only a collection-workflow problem.

### Gaps
- No post-2024 measurement of average DSO or payment days for Indian MSMEs found (Atradius PDF inaccessible; RBI and CIBIL data not located).
- No quantified evidence of 43B(h)'s effect on payment days, vendor delisting, or compliance; only headlines and explainers.
- Live Samadhaan pending total not readable (portal failed); no state or CPSE breakdown with date.
- Lok Sabha passage and assent of the 2026 Bill not verified.

## 2. MSME credit gap, formal credit share, interest rates, TReDS, Account Aggregator, ULI, GST-based lending

### Takeaway
The credit gap is confirmed and very large, but the latest authoritative figures are estimates with caveats (NITI Aayog: only 19% of MSME credit demand met formally by FY21, about Rs 80 lakh crore unmet). Formal credit has been growing (CIBIL-SIDBI: Rs 35.2 lakh crore, Mar 2025) and delinquency is low, so the claim that "banks reject moderate-score borrowers" is plausible but not measured here. The claim of "12-15% cost of debt" is only loosely supported by a lender blog; I found no independent rate survey. TReDS is real and growing fast, with Rs 3.47 lakh crore financed in FY2025-26 [vendor/press], but remains small against the problem. ULI and Account Aggregator are live but MSME-specific traction is thin.

### Cited Findings
- NITI Aayog report (released 2 May 2025): share of micro and small enterprises accessing credit via scheduled banks rose from 14% to 20% between 2020 and 2024; medium from 4% to 9%; only 19% of MSME credit demand was met formally by FY21, leaving an estimated Rs 80 lakh crore unmet — [PIB, 2 May 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126063)
- IFC estimate cited as Rs 16.66 lakh crore gap with more than 80% of MSMEs not receiving formal financing [PRE-2023, probably 2018 vintage; the source document is a WASME paper, date not confirmed] — [WASME/UNCITRAL paper](https://uncitral.un.org/sites/default/files/media-documents/EN/wasme_access_to_credit_for_indian_msmes.pdf). This conflicts by roughly 5x with NITI's Rs 80 lakh crore; the two use different definitions (IFC counts formal MSMEs; NITI appears to include all MSME demand) and should not be mixed.
- A journal study reports 87.38% of 633.88 lakh MSMEs excluded from institutional credit, with about 80 lakh units accessing it (undated secondary study, Odisha-focused) — [JMSR](https://www.jmsr-online.com/article/bridging-the-credit-gap-an-analytical-study-of-msme-financing-in-odisha-india-112/). Low-confidence; mixes in informal units.
- SIDBI-TransUnion CIBIL MSME Pulse (published 22 May 2025, data to 31 Mar 2025): MSME credit outstanding Rs 35.2 lakh crore (up from Rs 31 lakh crore in Mar 2024, +13%); commercial credit supply +3% in FY2024-25 but -11% YoY in Jan-Mar 2025 quarter; credit enquiries +11%; delinquency (sub-standard) 1.8%, a five-year low; delinquency was 5.8% in the up-to-Rs 10 lakh segment (up from 5.1%) and 2.9% in Rs 10-50 lakh; new-to-credit borrowers 47% of new originations (51% a year earlier) — [SIDBI press release](https://www.sidbi.in/head/uploads/pressrelease_documents/MSME_Pulse_PR_22_05_2025.pdf)
- Interest rates [VENDOR]: IIFL Finance blog (7 Jul 2026) gives no numeric bank or NBFC ranges; says unsecured MSME credit costs roughly 3-5 percentage points above secured, scores above 700 get the lower end, below about 650 attract a risk premium — [IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/business-loan/msme-loan-interest-rate-comparison). I did not obtain a credible dated source for "12-15%".
- TReDS volumes: invoice discounting rose from Rs 40,000 crore (FY2021-22) to Rs 3.47 lakh crore (FY2025-26) — [Angel One, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/government-mandates-treds-for-msme-invoice-settlement-by-cpses). RXIL alone financed Rs 1,21,105 crore in FY26 (+51%), crossed Rs 3 lakh crore cumulative (announced 10 Apr 2026), with 72,000+ MSMEs and 3,400+ corporate buyers onboarded and over 1.2 crore invoices financed — [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/rxil-treds-crosses-landmark-%E2%82%B9300000-crore-milestone-in-cumulative-invoice-financing/) [RXIL press, not independent]. Earlier: all TReDS financed 41.6 lakh invoices worth Rs 1.38 lakh crore in FY24 — [M1xchange](https://www.m1xchange.com/invoice-discounting-m1xchanges-transaction-volume-crosses-32000-crore-in-h1-fy25/) [operator source].
- TReDS mandates: companies above Rs 500 crore turnover plus CPSEs mandated in Nov 2018; threshold cut to Rs 250 crore in Nov 2024; all operating CPSEs must settle MSME invoices through TReDS effective 30 Jun 2026 per Budget 2026-27; five platforms operate (RXIL, M1xchange, Invoicemart, C2treds, DTX) — [Angel One, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/government-mandates-treds-for-msme-invoice-settlement-by-cpses)
- TReDS constraints (RXIL-commissioned impact assessment, May 2025): participation lowers receivables-to-sales by about 23 percentage points versus control firms; buyers struggle to get suppliers onboarded; low seller awareness; few financiers; non-MSME suppliers cannot onboard; many registered users inactive; export factoring not supported; discount rates vary by buyer rating, and over 60% of surveyed MSMEs joined for lower interest — [RXIL report](https://www.rxil.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/trade-receivables-impact-assessment-report-2025.pdf) [operator-commissioned]. One academic paper: as of 31 Mar 2025, 135,093 MSME sellers and 7,937 corporate buyers registered across the three original platforms (excerpt only) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6735318.pdf?abstractid=6735318&mirid=1)
- Account Aggregator: an estimated Rs 3.82 lakh crore of AA-enabled loans across 3.68 crore loans in FY26; AA is 8.4% of retail and MSME lending by value and 11.8% by volume; report expects GST, CBDT and EPFO data to strengthen cash-flow lending (report released 27 Aug 2026) — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/indias-account-aggregator-ecosystem-drives-rs-382-lakh-crore-loans-in-fy26--20260827133606)
- ULI: about 7.5 lakh loans worth Rs 38,000 crore since the Aug 2023 pilot (as of Jan 2025); 1.6 lakh MSME loans worth Rs 14,500 crore in the pilot — [Inc42, 24 Jan 2025](https://inc42.com/buzz/loans-worth-inr-38k-cr-extended-via-uli-so-far/). RBI reviewed rollout in Jul 2025 because adoption was "below expectations"; supply-chain finance use cases gained little traction — [Angel One, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/rbi-reviews-uli-rollout-with-lenders-amid-slow-lending-adoption). I found no 2026 ULI volume figure.

### Inferences
- "Startup and working capital access" is real but the strongest India evidence is about MSMEs and working capital, not startup equity. For a payments provider, the closest addressable slice is receivables-backed and cash-flow-backed lending, which payment/settlement data can feed.
- TReDS volume (Rs 3.47 lakh crore in FY26) is small next to the stock of overdue and unmet demand cited above, which suggests TReDS is a partial answer and is limited by buyer onboarding rather than demand.
- The "12-15% cost of debt" claim is unverified; I treat it as plausible for secured bank lending and likely understated for unsecured or small-ticket NBFC/fintech credit, but I have no cited source for that.

### Gaps
- No independent, dated MSME interest-rate survey (RBI weighted average lending rate for MSMEs not retrieved).
- No data on rejection rates for moderate-score borrowers.
- No 2026 ULI statistics; no direct GST-data-based lending volume.
- TReDS average discount rate in percent not found.
- The IFC India gap figure in a post-2023 source not found.

## 3. Reconciliation, bookkeeping and compliance burden; GST and e-invoicing; accounting tool adoption

### Takeaway
The "5-6 hours a week on reconciliation" claim is not confirmed for India by any independent source I found. The compliance load is real and rule-driven (e-invoicing at Rs 5 crore turnover, 30-day reporting window for Rs 10 crore and above), but I found no hard survey of hours spent. Adoption data for Tally/Zoho/Vyapar/Khatabook/Refrens was not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- E-invoicing applies to taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover of Rs 5 crore or more in any year since FY2017-18, in force since 1 Aug 2023 (phased from Rs 500 crore in Oct 2020); for turnover Rs 10 crore and above, invoices older than 30 days cannot be reported to the IRP from 1 Apr 2025; the rule continues in FY2026-27 — [Gimbooks blog](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/5-crore-e-invoice-turnover-rule-2026/amp/) [secondary; confirm against official notification 10/2023-Central Tax]
- Vendor-reported time saving: Zoho survey (Nov 2022) of 150 CAs and 350 Zoho Books users: 88% of CAs said cloud accounting saves up to 10 hours a week; 85% of businesses saved up to 10 hours; 75% said cash flow improved by collecting payments on time. Commissioned by Zoho, no methodology, "up to" claims [VENDOR; 2022, outside the 2023 window] — [IT Voice, 18 Nov 2022](https://www.itvoice.in/88-of-accountants-save-up-to-10-hours-a-week-using-cloud-accounting-software-finds-zohos-survey)
- Settlement reports lacking transaction-level linkage and missing bank UTRs make matching payouts to bank credits hard (qualitative list, no complaint data) [VENDOR] — [Razorpay blog, 11 Jun 2026](https://razorpay.com/blog/settlement-transparency-the-complete-merchant-playbook-for-questioning-your-payment-gateway-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Reconciliation pain is credible specifically where payment data is poor (net-only settlements, no UTR, bundled deductions). That is a payments-provider-fixable slice of the problem; general bookkeeping and expense categorisation are not.
- Mandatory e-invoicing and GST matching raise baseline workload for firms above Rs 5 crore, so the "5-6 hours" figure is more believable for that tier than for micro firms.

### Gaps
- No independent Indian survey of hours spent on reconciliation or bookkeeping.
- No adoption numbers for Tally, Zoho Books, Vyapar, Khatabook or Refrens (search returned vendor blogs only, and one 2019 IndiaMART item that I did not read).
- GSTN filing statistics and SME compliance-cost surveys not found.

## 4. Payment gateway pain in India: settlement, holds, reserves, chargebacks, failure rates, RBI rules

### Takeaway
Partly confirmed. Settlement timing is regulated (RBI Payment Aggregator Directions, 15 Sep 2025), and holds and unexplained deductions are a documented merchant complaint, but the evidence is qualitative and from gateway vendors. Silent webhook drops and "high-risk merchant" labelling were not evidenced with India data. UPI technical declines are now below 1%, so raw payment failure is not a major problem, though bank-level failure rates vary widely.

### Cited Findings
- RBI consolidated Payment Aggregator Directions dated 15 Sep 2025 repeal the 2020 PA/PG guidelines and 2023 cross-border PA directions; escrow must be ring-fenced and no commingling or netting; net worth Rs 15 crore at application and Rs 25 crore by year three; continuous merchant monitoring; cross-border PAs capped at Rs 25 lakh per transaction, and non-INR settlement only for directly onboarded Indian exporters — [IndiaCorpLaw, 9 Oct 2025](https://indiacorplaw.in/2025/10/09/decoding-rbis-overhaul-of-the-payment-aggregator-directions/)
- Settlement: a Razorpay (June 2026) blog says the directions require crediting merchants no later than T+1 banking day; UPI typically T+1, cards commonly T+2 working days in practice, longer across weekends and holidays; gateways may hold funds for volume spikes, chargebacks or suspected fraud — [Razorpay, 11 Jun 2026](https://razorpay.com/blog/settlement-transparency-the-complete-merchant-playbook-for-questioning-your-payment-gateway-in-2026/) [VENDOR; the T+1 claim was not confirmed in the IndiaCorpLaw summary, which gave no T+n figure, so verify against the RBI text]
- Merchant complaints listed (no data): delayed payouts, unexplained holds, no UTRs, bundled deductions, undisclosed cut-offs, unclear chargeback timing, hidden FX spreads and missing FIRCs for exporters — [Razorpay, Jun 2026](https://razorpay.com/blog/settlement-transparency-the-complete-merchant-playbook-for-questioning-your-payment-gateway-in-2026/) [VENDOR]
- UPI technical declines: 0.7-0.8% in Nov 2024, versus 8-10% in 2016 (NPCI CEO statement) — [Inc42, 20 Nov 2024](https://inc42.com/buzz/technical-glitches-on-upi-have-fallen-below-1). Feb 2026: highest business-failure rate among top-50 UPI participants was Airtel Payments Bank at 21.97%; highest technical decline Baroda UP Bank at 7.26%; the Centre asked banks and NPCI to reduce failures — [Outlook Money, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-cleanup-drive-upi-glitches-under-scanner-as-govt-seeks-higher-success-rates). UPI was 81% of retail digital payments in FY25 (same source).
- Penalties under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act can reach Rs 1 crore; 18% GST applies to MDR (Razorpay, Jun 2026, vendor).

### Inferences
- Because UPI is dominant and technically reliable, India merchants' payment pain is less about failed payments and more about settlement opacity, reconciliation data quality, holds and cross-border friction.
- Reserve-type holds exist in practice but I found no India percentage or duration data, so the "reserves" claim is unquantified for India.

### Gaps
- No quantified India data on rolling reserves, held-fund disputes, or high-risk merchant designation.
- No India data on silent webhook failures (this is an engineering-level issue and likely not reported in any survey).
- Chargeback rates for Indian merchants not found; the $100 billion chargeback figure in the Razorpay blog is global and unsourced.
- Instant-settlement adoption data not found.

## 5. Large businesses: treasury, AP/AR automation, vendor payment fraud, collections at scale, cross-border receivables

### Takeaway
Vendor-payment fraud via spoofed vendor email with changed bank details is confirmed in India with named cases, but aggregate India loss statistics were not found. Evidence on treasury and AR automation pain at large companies is thin. Cross-border receivables friction is real: RBI extended the export realisation window from 9 to 15 months, but I have no quantified default rates.

### Cited Findings
- Dr. Reddy's Laboratories lost about Rs 2.16 crore after a spoofed vendor email (lookalike address differing in letter casing) in an intercepted thread asked it to pay a new Bank of Baroda account; payment processed 3 Nov (2025), reported 9 Nov 2025; funds traced to Vadodara and split into sub-accounts; police froze the primary account — [The420.in](https://the420.in/?p=55259). Note the victim was the paying large company; the supplier's complaint triggered the case; Dr. Reddy's made no official statement.
- Other cases: a Pune employee booked for Rs 2.45 crore corporate fraud using fake CEO emails — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/pune/pune-employee-booked-for-245-crore-corporate-fraud-using-fake-ceo-emails) (headline only); Indore cyber police recovered over Rs 3 crore in a cross-border BEC scam — [The420.in](https://the420.in/indore-business-email-compromise-scam-recovery/) (headline only; dates not confirmed).
- Global BEC losses about US$2.9 billion in 2023 (FBI IC3, US-centred) — cited in [Winvesta, 18 May 2026](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/business-email-compromise-fraud-a-complete-guide-for-indian-exporters) [VENDOR; no India total given; the page says India is "frequently cited" among heavily targeted countries but offers no figure]
- RBI/FEMA: exporters now have 15 months (up from 9) to realise export proceeds, and advance-settlement timeline extended from one to three years; reasons were global trade disruption, US tariffs and compliance checks (article undated, likely late 2025) — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/trade/rbi-eases-export-repayment-rules-firms-face-global-trade-pressures/). No delay data.
- TReDS does not support export factoring (RXIL report, May 2025).
- Public-sector buyers: states and CPSEs are the largest named delayed-payment defaulters in the (undated) Samadhaan snapshot, and CPSE TReDS settlement became mandatory 30 Jun 2026 (sources in Section 1 and 2).

### Inferences
- Changed-bank-detail fraud is a real, verifiable-by-API problem (beneficiary name and account verification before payout), and Indian payment infrastructure (UPI/IMPS/penny-drop name match) can address it. That makes it one of the better payments-fit problems, but it affects large payers and exporters more than the small merchants in the Reddit threads.
- Large-business treasury and AP automation pain is plausible but I cannot rank it from evidence found.

### Gaps
- No aggregate India BEC or vendor-fraud loss statistic (RBI fraud reports, I4C, CERT-In not retrieved).
- No India data on large-company DSO, collections effort, or treasury automation adoption (searches for Big-4/McKinsey/EY/Bain content were not run to completion).
- No quantification of overseas customers failing to pay Indian exporters.

## 6. Verdict table: nine claims versus India evidence, size, and payments-provider fit

### Takeaway
Five of nine are confirmed or strongly plausible for India (late payments, cash-flow timing, credit access, reconciliation data quality, cross-border friction). The "56 vs 27 days" figure is US-only. Profitability attribution and SKU pricing have no India finance-pain evidence in this research, and are analytics problems that payments cannot fix. Payment infrastructure fits best on collection, settlement, payouts, verification and reconciliation.

### Cited Findings
(Verdicts rest on the cited findings in Sections 1-5; the "56 days vs 27 days" figure is a US claim from the brief and is not supported by any source I found for India.)

| # | Claim | India status | Size / evidence | Fixable by payments infra? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cash flow vs profit timing, payroll stress | Plausible, indirectly confirmed (late payments, 63% overdue, working-capital gap) | No direct India payroll-stress data found | Partly: faster collection and settlement help; timing mismatch itself is not fixable |
| 2 | Unpaid or delayed invoices | Confirmed. The "56 vs 27 days" figures are US-specific | Rs 55,244 crore claims (Aug 2026), 63% of credit sales overdue, 7% bad debt (Atradius, Oct 2024) | Partly: invoice-linked collection, reminders, payment links, TReDS rails. Cannot fix buyer's unwillingness or liquidity |
| 3 | Time spent chasing clients | No India hard data; plausible | Qualitative only | Yes, in part: collection links, automated reminders, status tracking |
| 4 | Access to capital; banks rejecting; cost of debt 12-15% | Credit gap confirmed (NITI: Rs 80 lakh crore, 19% of demand met formally, FY21); rate figure unverified; rejection claim unmeasured | Rs 35.2 lakh crore outstanding (Mar 2025), 1.8% delinquency | Weak: payment/settlement data can feed lenders (AA, GST), but capital itself is a lending problem |
| 5 | Reconciliation and categorisation 5-6 hours/week | Not independently confirmed; reconciliation difficulty tied to poor settlement data is credible | Only vendor survey (Zoho 2022) | Yes for payout/settlement reconciliation (UTR, transaction-level reports); No for expense categorisation |
| 6 | Attributing spend to results / true profitability | No India finance-pain evidence found | None | No |
| 7 | Pricing and margins across SKUs | No India evidence found | None | No |
| 8 | Gateway issues (holds, delays, reserves, webhooks, cross-border) | Partly confirmed: settlement opacity and holds are documented as complaints by vendors; RBI PA rules (Sep 2025) govern; UPI technical declines 0.7-0.8% (Nov 2024). Reserves and webhook drops not quantified | Qualitative | Yes, this is the provider's own domain |
| 9 | Overseas customers failing to pay | Plausible for exporters; RBI widened realisation window to 15 months. No default-rate data | None quantified | Partly: cross-border collection, FX, documentation (FIRC); not buyer credit risk |

### Inferences
- Best payments-fit problems, ranked by evidence strength: (a) late B2B payments and collection (large, well documented); (b) settlement and reconciliation transparency; (c) payout verification against changed-bank-detail fraud; (d) cross-border collection friction.
- Not fixable by payments alone: capital access, profitability analysis, SKU pricing, and the cash-flow timing gap itself.
- Caveat for the report writer: the Reddit threads come from AI-generated summaries and skew US; in India the dominant, best-evidenced issue is B2B delayed payment backed by law and government data, not payment-gateway failure.

### Gaps
- No India evidence located on claims 6 and 7 (absence of evidence from my searches, not proof they are absent).
- Claim 3 and claim 5 lack independent India surveys.
- Searches for Bain/BCG/McKinsey/EY India SME reports, RBI MSME lending rates, GSTN statistics, and Tally/Khatabook/Vyapar adoption were not completed in this session.
